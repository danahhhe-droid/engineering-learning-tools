INVESTIGATION 01

Where is the Sun relative to the panel?

Geographic location, season, and solar time set the Sun's direction. Tilt and panel azimuth determine the angle at which its rays arrive.

Set the scene

Solar noon is 12 here. This is solar time, not a conversion from the local clock. Day 80 ≈ March 21, 172 ≈ June 21, 355 ≈ December 21.

Azimuth convention: 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west. Some textbook diagrams use south as zero.

Gbeam,panel = DNI × max(0, cos θ)

θ is measured between the incoming Sun direction and the panel normal. A 60° incidence angle admits half the direct power per panel area compared with normal incidence.

The elevation sketch shows directions in a vertical plane. Azimuth also contributes to θ. Diffuse irradiance uses the isotropic-sky approximation. Ground reflection is omitted. Irradiance inputs remain fixed when time changes; this is geometry, not a weather forecast. At or below the horizon, the teaching model returns zero.

Try these comparisons

  2. Keep tilt 38.6° and change day 80 to 172 and 355. Then compare solar times 9, 12, and 15.
  3. Turn the panel north, then add diffuse irradiance. Can the front still receive diffuse light when direct incidence is unfavorable?
INVESTIGATION 02

How does a cell develop a voltage?

Separate three ideas: creating mobile carriers, separating them, and delivering power through a load.

Ephoton (eV) ≈ 1240 / λ (nm)
Silicon band gap in the textbook: 1.11 eV

Above-gap absorption is possible, not guaranteed. This schematic suppresses reflection, incomplete absorption, recombination, and transport details. Carrier motion is illustrative and not to scale.

Semiconductor reasoning

Silicon shares four valence electrons through covalent bonding. Donor dopants such as phosphorus provide electrons (n type). Acceptor dopants such as boron create holes (p type). The bulk material remains approximately electrically neutral.

Diffusion and recombination near the junction leave fixed positive donor ions on the n side and negative acceptor ions on the p side. The resulting field points from n to p. Electron force opposes the field; hole force follows it.

In the dark, the equilibrium built-in field alone does not supply continuous external power. Under illumination, carrier generation and separation establish a terminal photovoltage. An open circuit can have voltage while delivering zero power.

INVESTIGATION 03

Where does the cell deliver maximum power?

Current, voltage, and power describe different aspects of performance. The best load is an operating point on the curve.

I = Iph − I0[exp(qV/kT) − 1]
P = VI · E = Pt · η = Pmax / (GA)

Single-cell example: T = 298.15 K, ideality factor n = 1, I₀ = 10⁻¹⁰ A, Iph = 0.100 A at 1000 W/m², cell area A = 2.5 cm². No series or shunt losses. Fixed spectrum and temperature. Uniform irradiance scales photocurrent linearly. This is not a partial-shading or motor model.

Blue: current (left axis) · Gold: power (right axis) · Black dot: selected load · dashed line: maximum power

Textbook notation and maximum power

The textbook uses JL for delivered current density and Js for short-circuit current density. Here Iph is the generated current and I is the delivered current; I = JA. Do not mix current (A) and current density (A/m²).

At short circuit, V = 0 and P = 0. At open circuit, I = 0 and P = 0. Between them, maximize VI, or solve d(VI)/dV = I + V(dI/dV) = 0. The maximum-power rectangle is smaller than IscVoc.

Increasing uniform illumination raises current strongly and open-circuit voltage logarithmically in this fixed-temperature model. A motor connected directly is not guaranteed to operate at maximum power.

These are constant-condition, steady-speed estimates on a level surface. They omit startup, acceleration, changing illumination, slip and changes in the motor operating point.
